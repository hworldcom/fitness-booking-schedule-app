# MovX Club — Scheduling, messaging and group-training MVP specification

Last updated: 16 October 2026.

MovX is a focused scheduling product for martial-arts coaching. Guests discover platform-approved coaches or clearly labelled fictional demo coaches and their real published availability. Email-authenticated athletes request one capacity-one private session, and the coach confirms one athlete before a booking exists. Approved coaches manage their public profile, training locations, location-aware recurring one-hour availability, request queue and booked schedule.

The delivered scheduling foundation is extended by the adopted group-training target in section 15: direct registration, manual coach confirmation and Free or pay-at-session pricing. The full Events flow is not delivered yet. This project excludes blockchain, wallets, tokens, passes, credits, in-app payment collection, group funding, follows, posts and feeds. Historical excluded-product records do not restore those capabilities; Solana hackathon work belongs to the separate `fitness-booking-social-app` repository.

The adopted M8 [private-messaging target](#16-private-messages-and-session-notes) adds coach inquiries before requesting a session, ongoing pair conversations containing request cards and consent-based direct messages. Its runtime and local two-account validation are delivered under COR0022; hosted release remains separate. Individual booking notes and shared training plans remain separate COR0015 work.

### Proposed implementation defaults

The following are implementation defaults rather than additional user decisions:

- Home uses canonical `discipline`, `location` and `page` URL state, omitting page one and stripping unsupported parameters. Load more adds 15 per page in the same grid; out-of-range pages clamp to current results. Enhanced navigation preserves scroll/focus, while native GET remains usable without JavaScript. Home-only discipline case/alias normalization maps supported values such as BJJ; existing primary public-place/gym text matching and alphabetical directory order are reused.
- M9 materializes effective private availability for the current Europe/Berlin date and the following 29 local dates, excluding starts at/before database time. The complete operating defaults, including overrides and restoration, are in section 18; DEV0252 implements this backend authority while calendar/editor/public-navigation peers remain pending.
- Repeating a request while the same athlete already has an active attempt for that occurrence returns the existing request rather than creating a duplicate.
- Expiry may be reconciled transactionally during participant/public reads and request mutations for the first release; no background scheduler is required by the product contract.
- A coach or athlete cancellation records who cancelled and when; P0 does not require a reason field or notification delivery.
- Historical migrations may retain unused wallet/payment/event tables for safe forward compatibility. Current code does not read or expose them.
- Referenced training locations are deactivated rather than destructively deleted; changing the primary location is one atomic replacement.
- A location selected from optional provider search becomes bounded coach-owned profile data. It does not silently create a global gym or point-of-interest catalogue record.
- The location-aware calendar uses stable text cues in addition to color so every assigned venue remains understandable without color perception.
- Connection requests may be declined by the recipient or withdrawn by the sender; unsuccessful attempts and removed connections retain bounded lifecycle evidence. Reconnection requires a fresh request and explicit acceptance with current eligibility. Exact retry/resend bounds are recorded by the persistence ticket before implementation.
- Crossed connection requests recover the existing pending request and require explicit recipient acceptance; sending never silently accepts. One coach-profile/athlete-profile pair is distinct from the reverse teaching relationship when both accounts coach. Self and cross-dataset pairs are invalid.
- Approved/demo coaches may create or accept new connections with qualifying athletes even when their coach profile is hidden; suspended coaches retain existing relationship reads/removal and pending decline/withdrawal but create or accept no new connection. Discovery and rebooking still obey their existing visibility/approval rules.
- My coaches is reachable within My sessions, and My athletes within Coach workspace → Bookings; pending requests remain separate from accepted lists. These placements preserve existing primary navigation. Connection status is application-only, with no email or push promise.

## 3. Actors and authority

### Guest

A guest can browse the public story, coach list, coach profiles, confirmed locations and future open times. A guest cannot submit a request or mutate schedule state.

<a id="client"></a>

### Athlete

An athlete has a verified email-backed MovX account and application profile. The athlete can request one eligible future occurrence with another coach, view only their own pending-request status, upcoming confirmed sessions and retained history in `My sessions`, withdraw their own pending request, cancel their own future confirmed booking and optionally manage a private account avatar. A terminal unsuccessful attempt does not consume capacity and may be followed by a new attempt while that occurrence remains eligible. Ordinary athletes plus pending and rejected coach applicants receive `My sessions` as their private primary-navigation destination and no `Coach workspace` item. An account avatar does not become public coach media merely because the account later applies to coach.

### Coach

A coach retains athlete capability, so approved and fictional demo coaches keep `My sessions` and additionally receive `Coach workspace` in desktop and mobile navigation. Their main `Profile` item opens coach Profile by default, while the bottom-left and upper-right account controls continue to open the personal account Profile. Coach Profile, Schedule and Bookings share one paper-background coach-workspace heading with a truthful publication summary and one Profile-first sub-navigation at a stable position before the active view's content; Schedule contains the Calendar and Availability views in section 18, while Bookings alone contains the private paginated request queue, detailed booked schedule, authorized athlete identity/avatar and booking actions. After platform approval, the owner can publish their coach profile, manage multiple confirmed training locations, select one active primary location, optionally manage a separate public coach portrait, assign recurring availability to actual locations, accept or decline pending requests, close one unavailable occurrence, inspect confirmed bookings, cancel a future confirmed booking and mark an elapsed confirmed booking completed. A pending or rejected applicant may prepare a private draft but has no public coach or scheduling authority. A suspended coach receives the same two private destinations, with bounded Bookings access needed to resolve existing confirmed sessions, but accepts no pending request, receives no new request and makes no public-media change. A coach cannot see unrelated requests, athlete bookings or private identity data.

### Platform reviewer

Until the internal admin panel exists, an authorized operator uses a guarded owner-only command to approve, reject or suspend an exact coach application. Every decision records the expected prior state, reviewer/source, bounded reason, verification-policy version and immutable audit event. Ordinary application/runtime roles cannot review applications or grant coach authority.

### MovX service

MovX verifies the Supabase session, establishes transaction-local actor context, enforces row-level authorization and performs bounded database mutations. It does not invent coaches, availability, requests or bookings when configuration/database access is unavailable.

## 4. Primary user flows

### 4.1 Register as an athlete or apply to coach

1. A visitor requests a one-time email code and verifies it through Supabase Auth.
2. MovX creates or restores the same minimal application profile.
3. The visitor follows the `Find a coach` path and can use athlete behavior immediately, or follows `Become a coach` and submits a coach application.
4. A pending applicant may prepare a hidden coach-profile draft but cannot publish availability or receive booking requests.
5. A guarded platform review approves, rejects or suspends the application atomically with audit evidence.
6. Approval unlocks coach publication and scheduling without removing athlete capability, and primary navigation adds `Coach workspace` alongside `My sessions`. Approval does not automatically make the profile visible.
7. The account may manage a private avatar from Account Profile. An approved coach separately chooses whether to add a public portrait to the coach profile.

### 4.2 Discover a coach and time

1. A guest opens Home and sees a compact introduction, prominent discipline/location search, scrollable discipline shortcuts and one alphabetical public coach grid. All disciplines is selected by default, with up to 15 matching coaches shown initially. Explanation and coach-application content follow discovery.
2. Home applies discipline/location values to its own grid through a GET form and canonical Home URLs, with typed supported discipline suggestions and JavaScript-free recovery. Search, discipline shortcuts and clear filters reset pagination. Load more expands the same grid while further matches exist and preserves applied filters/reload/history. Cards show public portraits or initials, identity, disciplines and the primary training place; profile links open actual published schedules. Explore retains its separate normalized `q`, discipline/location and list/map URLs. Unsupported date, weekly availability, proximity, price, rating and review controls do not appear.
3. Explore shares Home’s typography, pill search, typed/scrollable discipline controls and simple public portrait/initial card styling, with a compact heading and retained coach/focus query. Full biographies remain on coach profiles; compact today summaries retain actual venues and explicit no-availability states. Explore returns the complete server-backed filtered list of visible coaches. Desktop places that list beside the primary-location map; mobile defaults to list and uses canonical `view=list|map` state without losing filters or browser navigation.
4. OpenFreeMap-backed discovery shows each visible coach once at that coach's confirmed active primary location without an application map key. Map failure returns or retains the complete list and profile links.
5. The primary marker describes the coach's public training base, not live presence or today's venue. Explore has no date selector and keeps the coach visible when there is no open time today; any today summary labels the actual occurrence locations or reports `No availability today`.
6. A coach profile shows bounded public details, an optional public portrait, every confirmed active training location with the primary one labelled, and future open dated one-hour occurrences grouped or labelled by actual location. One provider-neutral map fits all active locations; distinct matching marker/list colors and numeric labels aid comparison without replacing names and addresses, and map failure leaves the list usable. Missing portraits use initials rather than a fabricated image.
7. No placeholder coach, location or time is shown when persistent directory or availability data is empty or unavailable.

### 4.3 Request a private session

1. A signed-in athlete selects one open future occurrence belonging to another visible coach. No fixed minimum-notice interval applies.
2. One authorized database operation rechecks actor/dataset/coach/time/status boundaries and creates or recovers that athlete's active pending request. It sets the decision deadline to the earlier of 12 hours after the database submission time or occurrence start, and leaves the occurrence open.
3. The athlete sees `Request saved`, `Awaiting coach confirmation`, `This session is not confirmed yet` and the concrete decision deadline in `Europe/Berlin`. The response contains no other candidate identity, count or status.
4. Other eligible athletes may independently request the same occurrence. No pending request holds capacity, and coach-facing result sets remain bounded through pagination even though no candidate cap applies.
5. Reload derives the same active request from PostgreSQL. The athlete can withdraw it; after withdrawal, expiry, coach decline or non-selection, the athlete may create a new attempt only if the occurrence is still open and future.

### 4.4 Decide booking requests

1. The owning coach opens the private paginated request queue in Bookings and reviews requests grouped by occurrence.
2. The coach may decline one pending request, accept one pending request or close that one occurrence when no longer available.
3. Acceptance locks the occurrence and active requests together, creates one confirmed capacity-one booking for the selected athlete, marks the occurrence booked, accepts that request and closes every other pending request as not selected in one transaction. Those athletes see neutral `Not confirmed` or `Request closed` presentation rather than competition-oriented wording.
4. Decline terminalizes only that attempt. It leaves the occurrence and other pending requests open, and the same athlete may submit another eligible attempt.
5. Closing the occurrence marks it withdrawn and closes all pending requests as unavailable in one transaction. It does not edit the recurring availability rule or create a booking.
6. A request that reaches its decision deadline expires without consuming capacity. That deadline is normally 12 hours after submission but is capped at occurrence start for a late request. Concurrent accept/decline/withdraw/expire/close operations resolve to one authoritative idempotent outcome.

### 4.5 Cancel a booking

1. The owning athlete or coach selects a future confirmed booking.
2. One authorized operation records the cancellation actor/time and releases the reservation. Reopen only if the occurrence still satisfies the effective availability, horizon and coach-wide conflict checks in section 18; a retained booking on a day off must not reopen public time after cancellation.
3. Repeating the same cancellation returns the same terminal outcome; a different or unauthorized actor cannot mutate it. Reopening the occurrence never revives earlier request attempts, so athletes must submit new requests while it remains eligible.

### 4.6 Complete a session

1. After the scheduled start, the owning coach can mark a confirmed booking completed.
2. Completion is terminal and idempotent. Athletes and unrelated coaches cannot complete it.
3. P0 does not claim independent attendance proof or resolve service disputes.

### 4.7 Manage locations and publish availability

1. An approved coach adds or confirms one or more provider-neutral training locations and selects exactly one active location as primary before publishing the profile.
2. In Schedule → Availability, the coach edits weekday switches and whole-hour time ranges with one active owned venue per range, copies days deliberately and composes the complete weekly draft locally.
3. One explicit authorized save validates ownership, active-location state, the expected baseline and per-coach time overlap before replacing the complete recurring rule set and synchronizing effective dated occurrences once. Date overrides replace selected whole dates across venues; booked sessions remain with a warning. Section 18 defines closure, restoration and conflict behavior.
4. Every dated occurrence copies the actual assigned location snapshot. Changing the primary location never moves an existing occurrence or booking.
5. Deactivating a location stops new availability there, withdraws eligible future open occurrences and closes their pending requests while preserving booked and terminal history.

### 4.8 Participate as an athlete

Participant-facing website labels use athlete terminology consistently. Personal account labels, onboarding paths, public explanations and coach request/booking rows reflect that vocabulary. Coaches retain My sessions for the sessions they request as athletes and Coach workspace for the sessions they teach; identity and authorization remain unchanged.

### 4.9 Connect after a confirmed session

1. A coach and athlete who share at least one confirmed or completed booking can open Connect from their authorized session context. A pending booking request or cancelled-only history is insufficient.
2. Either participant sends a connection request. The server verifies their exact pair and qualifying booking state; sending creates no accepted relationship.
3. The recipient explicitly accepts or declines; the sender may withdraw. Acceptance rechecks that at least one shared booking is still confirmed or completed, so cancellation can invalidate a pending request unless another qualifying session remains.
4. Acceptance adds one pair to My coaches and My athletes, separately from incoming/outgoing requests and booking history. More confirmed sessions do not create duplicate connections.
5. The coach sees only that connected athlete's name, current photo or initials and pair-owned session history. The athlete sees the retained coach identity, its available public portrait and shared sessions; public-profile/rebooking shortcuts obey existing visibility and approval.
6. An accepted connection persists across cancellation/completion. Either participant may disconnect; removal revokes connection-derived data/media access without deleting or resetting requests, bookings or retained shared content. Exact participants retain their independent request/session permissions.
7. Reconnecting requires a fresh request and explicit acceptance with current eligibility. The relationship history view restores all retained shared requests and bookings, including declined, withdrawn, expired and not-selected requests and cancelled sessions. M8 opens the same ongoing pair conversation with the original request/booking card selected; it never duplicates accepted-request content into a second chat. COR0015 plans/notes remain attached to each original booking, with cancelled-session notes read-only and original author permissions retained. A terminal request does not by itself close the ongoing conversation; current context permissions follow section 16. Coach-private notes never enter shared history.

### 4.10 Create and join group training

M7 adds the separately owned [group-training flow](#15-group-training-and-events): coach draft/review/publication, direct athlete seat registration, manual coach confirmation/cancellation and private group history. Private occurrence requests and bookings retain the preceding flows.

## 5. Scheduling state model

### Application profile

One profile belongs to one verified Supabase Auth identity inside a dataset. It stores the bounded display identity used for authorization and presentation.

### Coach application and review

One current coach application belongs to one application profile. Absence means not applied; submitted applications move through `pending`, `approved`, `rejected` or `suspended`. Review events are append-only evidence of platform decisions. Historical self-service activation may remain stored for continuity but grants no current coach authority.

### Coach profile and training locations

One owner-scoped coach profile contains public name, slug, biography, disciplines, visibility, one scheduling timezone and an optional public portrait reference. It owns one or more confirmed provider-neutral training-location records; each record has stable identity, kind, public label, coordinates, provenance and active/inactive lifecycle, and may optionally reference a reviewed gym. Exactly one active location is primary while the profile is visible. That primary record supplies the coach's single Explore marker and public training-base label, but it does not establish live presence, choose an occurrence venue or grant gym/account authority.

Pending/rejected applicants may keep only a private draft; a user-created profile enters public discovery only while its application is approved, its owner selects visible and one confirmed active primary location exists. Provider-search selection stores only the bounded coach-owned location the coach explicitly confirms and does not populate a global point-of-interest catalogue. Referenced locations are deactivated rather than destructively deleted so scheduling history remains resolvable.

### Profile images

Image bytes live in Supabase Storage rather than PostgreSQL or the Vercel deployment filesystem. PostgreSQL stores only bounded source/path/version metadata. Private account avatars and public coach portraits use separate storage/access contracts; attaching an image never grants account or coach authority. Signed-in account presentation—including the desktop bottom-left account control, upper-right header control and Profile—prefers its private avatar and otherwise may display the owner's already-public coach portrait as a read-only fallback. A regular account therefore shows its private avatar or initials, while a coach can additionally fall back to the public portrait. Athlete `My sessions` cards may project the booked coach's current public portrait and link its complete visible identity to the existing public coach profile; a missing, removed or unreadable portrait retains initials. The owning coach may retrieve a request participant's current private avatar through a request-scoped authenticated application route for as long as that request record is retained, including terminal recent-request history. The owning coach of a confirmed booking may separately retrieve that booking athlete's current private avatar through a booking-scoped route while the booking remains confirmed. Public callers, athletes and unrelated coaches receive no image bytes, and the browser never receives the private Storage object path. These fallbacks and bounded reads do not copy media between buckets or change which endpoint removes each image.

JPEG, PNG and WebP source selections are accepted for the first release. The browser corrects orientation, square-crops and downsizes before upload; the server treats that output as untrusted, validates and re-encodes one canonical 512 × 512 WebP at quality 82, strips metadata and retains no original. The selected source is limited to 8 MiB and the canonical upload to 512 KiB. Replacement writes a new immutable object path before retiring the prior owned object so content-delivery-network caches do not retain an overwritten image. Removal restores the initials fallback.

### Coach–athlete connection

A connection is a private lasting relationship for one coach-profile/athlete-profile pair within the same dataset. A request records the sender and recipient; only its recipient accepts or declines, and only its sender withdraws. Its lifecycle is `Pending → Accepted`, `Pending → Declined` or `Pending → Withdrawn`; either accepted participant may transition `Accepted → Removed`. A fresh request may reconnect an eligible pair after a terminal outcome, while old attempts remain terminal. Crossed sends return the existing pending request rather than implying consent.

Pair identity, participant IDs, request/attempt identity, lifecycle state, creation/update/decision timestamps and decision/removal actor are the minimum authority data. PostgreSQL enforces one active pending/accepted pair and rechecks a shared confirmed/completed booking at sending and acceptance. A later cancellation never removes an already accepted connection. A different teaching direction between two coach accounts is a distinct pair; self and cross-dataset pairs fail closed.

Accepted projections contain only participant identity, bounded current presentation and sessions shared by that exact pair. The coach receives an authenticated connection-scoped opaque URL for the athlete's private avatar. Pending/declined/withdrawn/removed connections grant no new private-media or session-history access. Removal revokes the connection permission on the next read, with caching that cannot preserve former access; separate request/booking permissions remain intact. Goals, biographies and public athlete profiles are not added. Connection-only delivery does not implement messaging; M8 defines its separate contact permissions and still promises no notification delivery.

Disconnecting is a relationship transition, not a content-deletion operation. A fresh accepted attempt for the same pair restores access to a complete bounded history view of retained shared requests/bookings and, after COR0015 delivery, their booking-bound shared plans and notes. M8 conversation history is retained independently of connection state; renewed direct-message consent follows section 16. Retrieval may be paginated but must permit access to all retained records rather than silently dropping older history. Each request/booking and conversation keeps its original stable identifier, authors, timestamps, status and permissions; reconnection neither reopens terminal records nor copies content into a new request or booking. Independent participant access survives disconnection. Recovery includes unsuccessful request cards and cancelled-session content, excludes coach-private and unrelated content, and does not bypass qualifying-session/coach eligibility or future separately adopted account-retention policy.

### Recurring availability and dated occurrence

A recurring weekly rule belongs to exactly one active training location owned by the same coach and creates deterministic dated one-hour occurrences in the coach's single scheduling timezone. One weekday/time cell belongs to at most one location. An occurrence is `open`, `booked` or `withdrawn`, carries the immutable snapshot of the rule's actual location and belongs to exactly one coach. An open occurrence may have multiple independent pending requests without being held or booked. Overlapping rules/occurrences are rejected across all of the coach's locations. Changing which location is primary does not change a rule or occurrence. M9 adds versioned whole-date overrides and distinguishes availability-policy closures from manual occurrence closures; effective hours, replacement identities and recovery are defined in section 18.

### Booking request

A booking request is one athlete's attempt to receive coach confirmation for one occurrence. The same athlete has at most one active request for that occurrence, while terminal attempts remain immutable history and do not prevent a later eligible attempt. There is no per-occurrence candidate cap; private coach queries use bounded pagination. Its lifecycle is:

```text
Pending -> Accepted
Pending -> Declined
Pending -> Withdrawn
Pending -> Expired
Pending -> Not selected
Pending -> Occurrence closed
```

`Accepted` creates and links exactly one confirmed booking. Accepting one request makes every other pending request for that occurrence internally `Not selected`; closing or otherwise withdrawing the occurrence makes them `Occurrence closed`. Athlete presentation uses neutral `Not confirmed`, `Request closed` or `Session unavailable` language and never describes competition. All other terminal outcomes consume no capacity. Minimum request fields are request/attempt ID, occurrence/coach/athlete IDs, scheduled time and actual location snapshot, status, creation/update timestamps, decision deadline capped at occurrence start, decision actor/time and an optional accepted-booking ID. The athlete projection contains only that athlete's attempt and never exposes other request identifiers, identity, count, status or content.

### Private booking

A private booking is created only when the owning coach accepts a pending request and binds that occurrence, coach profile and selected athlete profile. Its lifecycle is:

```text
Confirmed -> Cancelled
Confirmed -> Completed
```

Minimum projection fields are booking ID, occurrence ID, coach/athlete IDs, scheduled start/end, actual occurrence-location snapshot, status, creation/update timestamps, cancellation actor/time and completion time. It contains no wallet, pass, credit, token, price, transaction signature or chain reference.

### Group session and registration

M7 introduces separate group activity, registration and coach time-reservation records. Their lifecycle, frozen terms and atomic authority are defined in [section 15](#15-group-training-and-events); they do not reuse private booking IDs or the historical funding projection tables.

### Private conversation and message consent

M8 adds one dataset/coach-profile/athlete-profile conversation, stable message and request-card identities, recipient read positions, separate direct-message consent attempts and pair-level block records. Context-specific send authority and the block/booking exception are defined in [section 16](#16-private-messages-and-session-notes). Messages neither establish a connection nor allocate a session. COR0015 session-content records remain booking-bound.

## 7. Requests, booking and recovery

Request creation, withdrawal, decline, expiry, acceptance, occurrence closure, booking cancellation and completion are atomic database operations with stable UUID identity and explicit actor context. Start/deadline checks use database instants, not the browser clock. The active-booking uniqueness rule prevents double confirmation even when several requests exist or coach actions race.

A request is eligible only when the occurrence is open, belongs to another visible coach, starts after authoritative database time and falls within the effective 30-day publication policy in section 18. No fixed minimum notice applies because only coach acceptance assigns capacity. Creation fixes its deadline at `least(created_at + 12 hours, scheduled_start_at)`, so a late request never remains pending after training begins. The Berlin-only interface renders these instants in `Europe/Berlin`, including daylight-saving changes, without changing stored authority.

A repeated same-athlete submission returns that athlete's active request. A terminal unsuccessful attempt—withdrawn, expired, declined, not selected or closed with the occurrence—remains history but permits a new attempt while the occurrence is still open and eligible. No candidate cap applies. Coach request queries are paginated, and athlete projections never contain another request or aggregate candidate count.

Acceptance locks the occurrence and relevant requests in deterministic order, creates one confirmed booking, marks the selected request accepted and occurrence booked, and closes every other pending request as not selected. Coach occurrence closure uses the same concurrency boundary to withdraw that occurrence and close its pending requests without selecting anyone. Decline closes one attempt; withdrawal belongs only to its requesting athlete; bounded reconciliation expires overdue requests. Every mutation returns the state that actually won rather than false success.

Cancellation locks the confirmed booking/occurrence together, records the authorized actor and frees the reservation. It reopens only a future occurrence still allowed by the current effective hours, actual venue, publication horizon and coach-wide conflicts; a day off or replacement venue cannot be bypassed by cancellation. It does not revive earlier request attempts; interested athletes submit new requests if the reopened occurrence is still open and future. Completion locks the booking and requires the owning coach plus an elapsed scheduled start. Terminal request and booking transitions are idempotent and cannot be reversed.

Connection send/accept operations recheck exact participants, current coach capability and a shared confirmed/completed booking inside the database mutation. Booking cancellation and connection acceptance must serialize their qualifying-state check so a race cannot accept a pair that lacks a qualifying booking at the winning acceptance instant. Duplicate/crossed sends, replayed decisions and accept/remove races return the authoritative attempt/pair state; stale request identifiers cannot accept a newer attempt. Connection actions never alter session capacity, booking state or request eligibility.

For M8, a saved request and its unique message card are committed or recovered together. Direct-message consent is independent of connection acceptance. Blocking and concurrent send/request/accept/connection decisions serialize through a common participant-pair guard; booking-related exceptions use immutable booking identities and database time. See section 16 for exact scope. No mail integration failure may leave a successful request response with a missing card.

## 10. Security and failure invariants

- Actor context is derived from a verified session and set transaction-locally.
- Direct table permissions remain least privilege; security-definer functions validate actor context and exact identifiers.
- Coach approval/rejection/suspension is unavailable to ordinary runtime roles. Decisions are expected-state guarded and atomically append immutable review evidence.
- Editable profile fields, Auth metadata, URL parameters and historical activation timestamps never establish approval or badge state.
- Private capacity-one enforcement exists in PostgreSQL, not only in browser button state; group maximum capacity and per-athlete active registration are separate database constraints.
- One active request per athlete/occurrence and one confirmed booking per occurrence are enforced in PostgreSQL, not only in interface state.
- Acceptance and occurrence closure lock the occurrence and relevant requests deterministically so concurrent decisions cannot produce two winners or partial closure.
- Cross-run/cross-coach location identifiers, hidden coaches, self-requesting, elapsed/non-open occurrences and invalid lifecycle transitions fail closed.
- PostgreSQL enforces one active primary location for a visible coach and prevents overlapping active occurrences across all of that coach's locations; browser colors, selectors and marker state are not authority.
- Mutation errors return bounded messages without database/Auth internals.
- Browser inputs are normalized and bounded; server/domain validation is authoritative.
- Uploaded images reject unsupported types and oversized/malformed data, are decoded and re-encoded server-side with metadata removed, and use immutable owner-scoped object paths. Browser-reported names, types and dimensions are not trusted.
- Private request/booking queries are actor-scoped and reveal only necessary identity/display/time/location/status fields. Athlete responses contain no other-candidate record or aggregate count; unbounded candidate lists are never returned in one response.
- Hosted builds reject loopback/incomplete database/Auth configuration without printing values.
- Pair uniqueness, explicit recipient consent and qualifying-booking checks for connections are enforced by PostgreSQL, including cancellation/acceptance races.
- Connection-derived private data/media is revocable on removal, and bounded projections reveal neither another pair nor private coach-note metadata.

## 15. Group training and events

This is the adopted M7 target. DEV0237/DEV0238 deliver its backend data/lifecycle, guarded publication and schedule integration. DEV0240 delivers the public/athlete interface and local proof; coach management, final combined rehearsal and deployment remain separate as recorded above. A **group session** is one coach-led activity with several reserved participant seats at one time and venue. It is separate from a capacity-one private booking, a recurring availability rule, a coach–athlete connection and the former funding events.

### 15.1 Confirmed choices and implementation defaults

Confirmed by the user: coaches create dated sessions with minimum/maximum participants; athletes discover and join; the initial model supports Free and paid-at-session sessions; joining reserves a seat directly, while the owning coach confirms or cancels manually. Reaching the minimum never confirms automatically.

The following are adopted implementation defaults, not additional user decisions:

- One dated activity, one owning approved/demo coach and one of that coach's active confirmed training locations. Date/start/end are in Europe/Berlin, with a positive duration within one local calendar date; nonexistent or ambiguous daylight-saving times fail validation. Recurring/multi-day events and co-hosts are outside M7.
- Minimum and maximum are integers with `1 <= minimum <= maximum <= 100`. A coach chooses a decision/registration deadline strictly after publication time and before start; an untouched open session becomes Not confirmed at that deadline. This expires an undecided promise rather than treating it as coach confirmation or coach cancellation.
- EUR is the initial display currency. Free is zero; a paid per-person amount is positive integer euro cents within the supported database integer range. The posted price includes required participation/venue costs. The coach collects at the venue; the application neither collects nor verifies payment and shows no paid/unpaid ledger, payout or refund workflow.
- Joining closes at the decision deadline even if the coach confirmed earlier. Athletes may withdraw until start; a confirmed session stays confirmed after a count drop and the owner remains responsible for an explicit cancellation if needed. No waitlist or minimum override exists.
- Publication freezes date/time, deadline, actual venue snapshot, minimum/maximum and price, along with the reviewed public description. A correction requires cancellation and a new draft; existing registrations never transfer automatically. Drafts remain editable/deletable until publication.
- Status is in-app only. No email/push reminder or guaranteed automatic background execution is promised. Deadline outcomes are reconciled atomically on current reads/mutations using database time; an elapsed or deadline-past session can never be joined or newly confirmed because a browser is stale.
- Public event discovery is a list with discipline/place text filters, bounded pagination and start-time/ID ordering; no group map, radius, ranking, price filter or new Home/Explore inventory is required. New public Events navigation and a separate athlete Group sessions destination reuse the delivered visual system.

### 15.2 Coach create, publish and decide

The owner drafts a title, bounded description, discipline/intended level, owned venue, local date/start/end, decision deadline, minimum/maximum and Free or pay-at-session price. Publication rechecks current approval/visibility, active owned location, future valid times and cross-product schedule conflicts, then stores frozen reviewed terms and the actual venue snapshot. A draft is not public inventory and blocks no time.

A published session is initially **Waiting for coach confirmation** and reserves coach time while collecting registrations. The owner can confirm only while the minimum is currently met and database time is before the decision deadline. Reaching the threshold only enables that action. Confirmation stays stable if later withdrawals lower the count. The coach can cancel a future open or confirmed session; cancellation terminates every active registration once and releases group time.

No explicit owner decision before the deadline yields **Not confirmed** regardless of participant count. The outcome is terminal; the owner cannot revive it after the deadline. Existing participant history remains available with truthful cancelled/not-confirmed/elapsed labels. An elapsed activity is presented as **Ended**; this is not proof that training took place, payment occurred or anyone attended.

### 15.3 Joining, leaving and privacy

Guests can view published upcoming activity details, actual venue/time, the decision deadline, fixed pricing, coach identity/trust label and aggregate active-registration counts. A guest signs in by the existing email flow before joining. One account may reserve one active seat per session; the owner cannot register for their own session, while other coaches can participate as athletes through their ordinary account. Current coach eligibility/visibility, dataset, time and capacity are rechecked in the mutation.

Before confirmation, the action/result says that a seat is reserved and the coach has not yet confirmed the session. A duplicate submission recovers the existing registration. Withdrawal is permitted only to that athlete before start, releases one seat and retains the withdrawn attempt; rejoining creates a fresh eligible registration while joining is still open. At or after start, no new join, withdrawal, confirmation or cancellation changes the recorded activity.

Public/other-athlete views never disclose participant identities or registration IDs. The owning coach has a bounded paginated name/initials roster; this grants no email or private-avatar access. An athlete sees only their own group registrations/history. Group registration does not create a private booking, qualify a coach–athlete connection, expose another athlete's schedule or grant session notes/private media. Hiding or suspending a coach blocks discovery/new joins/publication/confirmation; exact participants keep existing history/withdrawal rights and the owner may cancel future sessions. An already confirmed session is not silently erased by profile hiding or a location/profile edit.

### 15.4 Data and atomic lifecycle

New scheduling-owned group records store stable IDs, dataset/owner, frozen published terms/venue, event state, active capacity, decision/cancellation actor/time and bounded revision/timestamps. Registrations store exact session/athlete/attempt IDs, lifecycle and timestamps. Legacy funding tables and projections are not read, rebound or silently repurposed.

The session lifecycle is `Draft -> Open -> Confirmed`, `Open -> Cancelled`, `Confirmed -> Cancelled`, or `Open -> Not confirmed`. Ended is an elapsed presentation of retained history, not a coach attendance/completion mutation. Registrations transition from Active to Withdrawn, Session cancelled or Session not confirmed, retaining earlier attempts. Direct registration and session confirmation are distinct operations.

PostgreSQL enforces maximum capacity, one active registration per session/account, actor/dataset scope and terminal outcomes. Join/withdraw/confirm/cancel/deadline reconciliation locks the same session and affected registrations in a deterministic order. A confirm-versus-withdraw race either confirms with the minimum met at the winning instant or reports the actual under-minimum state; once confirmed, a later withdrawal does not reverse it. Exact retries are idempotent; stale attempts cannot mutate a replacement registration. No browser count or timer is authority.

### 15.5 Group and private coach time

Open and confirmed future group sessions reserve that coach's full `[start, end)` interval across all locations. Adjacent intervals do not overlap. Publication rejects any overlapping nonwithdrawn private occurrence/confirmed booking or active group session. The coach must explicitly close conflicting private availability through the existing tools first, preserving their known pending-request closure behavior; publication never overwrites a booking or closes private requests implicitly.

Group publication, private occurrence writes/acceptance/reopening and recurring materialization share one coach-wide database concurrency guard. Materialization skips a date/time blocked by an active group reservation without deleting its weekly rule. Cancellation/Not confirmed releases the group reservation; later materialization may create eligible previously uncreated private time, but an already withdrawn dated occurrence stays withdrawn and terminal private requests never revive. All actual venue snapshots remain stable when the primary location changes or a venue is edited/deactivated. This M7 contract adds no athlete-wide availability guarantee. M9 section 18 separately adds the coach private/group calendar overlay without changing group lifecycle ownership.

### 15.6 Surfaces and ownership

Public `/events` and `/events/[slug]` provide discovery/shareable detail. Coach workspace -> Events at `/coach/events` owns draft/review/roster/decision actions. My sessions links to a distinct Group sessions view at `/sessions/groups`; private booking/request presentation remains intact.

DEV0235 owns only its offline Explore preview; DEV0236 owns Home-aligned Explore coach discovery. Neither implements this extension. DEV0240 owns Events public navigation and group surfaces and may reuse stable shared components after the active Explore edits. No group entries or prices are added to Home/Explore coach results. COR0021's direct peers own data, conflict protection, coach UI, public/athlete UI and integrated proof separately; the legacy hackathon branch supplies no current behavior.

### 15.7 Delivery and verification

M7 is complete only when its direct runtime tickets and the integrated local acceptance proof pass. The acceptance matrix in section 13 includes the group scenarios. A real approved/demo owner and multiple local athletes must demonstrate free and pay-at-session registrations, manual minimum confirmation, a missed decision, withdrawal before/after confirmation, cancellation, last-seat/retry races, private-time conflicts, participant privacy and mobile/desktop/keyboard/unavailable recovery. The activity may be confirmed with four reservations, fall to three after withdrawal, and remain confirmed until its owner explicitly cancels; payment/attendance are never inferred.

Contract and ticket completion alone is not runtime or deployment completion. Hosted operations remain separate, and in-app payment, waitlists, recurring events, notifications, attendance tracking and group-derived connections need their own later contracts.

### 16.1 Contact and consent

An **inquiry** is a private question an authenticated athlete initiates from a visible approved/demo coach's public profile. It requires neither a selected occurrence, a saved booking request nor an accepted connection. The coach may reply. A saved session request likewise establishes its exact pair's service communication and creates its original request card. A coach cannot use this public contact path to approach arbitrary athletes. Guests sign in first and return to compose; sign-in alone never sends a message.

Inquiry/request/booking communication and **general direct messages** are separate permissions, displayed in one ongoing conversation. Service communication concerns an exact saved inquiry, request or booking; general messages do not require choosing a session. An existing accepted connection permits either participant to initiate general messages. An eligible unconnected pair instead uses a **message request**: one bounded opening message is presented to its recipient, who explicitly accepts or declines. Sending, reading, replying through a different service context or sending a crossed request never silently accepts it. Acceptance permits general chat, not a connection, booking, athlete profile or private-media access.

For MovX, the adopted eligibility default for unconnected general contact is a shared confirmed/completed private booking, matching its existing relationship boundary. An accepted connection qualifies independently if its booking was later cancelled. Pending booking requests, cancelled-only history without prior consent, group attendance, discovery and arbitrary account search grant no general-message outreach. They do not remove the independent athlete-to-visible-coach inquiry path.

Recheck that eligibility at message-request creation and acceptance. Cancellation before acceptance may invalidate an unconnected pending attempt; an already accepted message consent survives later booking completion/cancellation until connection removal, block or account/capability restrictions revoke it. These checks serialize with the relevant booking transition.

One active message-consent attempt exists per directed pair. The sender can withdraw; only the exact recipient can accept/decline. A crossed send recovers the pending attempt and offers the authorized recipient decision. Decline/withdrawal stays terminal; a later eligible request has a fresh attempt ID. Both participants may read their retained history without accepting a new request.

### 16.2 One conversation, explicit context

The adopted identity default is one conversation per dataset, coach profile and athlete profile. A reverse teaching relationship between two coach accounts is separate and clearly labelled. Self/cross-dataset conversations fail. Existing connection/history identity is preserved rather than merged into a new human-pair identifier.

Messages reference an exact service context or general-message consent. The composer displays that context, especially when general chat is paused or only booking communication is available. The server verifies the referenced entity, participants, dataset and current permission on every send. An inquiry has a stable saved identity with no fabricated slot/time. Contacting the same coach reuses the ongoing pair conversation. Connection acceptance alone need not create an empty inbox item; opening/sending creates or recovers it atomically.

Each booking request has one stable card keyed by its original request ID, showing actual venue/time, decision deadline and current authoritative status. Acceptance links its original booking; later requests create separate cards in the same conversation. The card updates in place, and each later participant-visible request/booking transition appends one status activity keyed by its original entity/revision, referring to the card without duplicating it. Incoming activities participate in recipient unread state; an actor's own decision is not incoming work for that actor. Activities use the actual persisted transition/revision, never a browser prediction. Historical bootstrap uses original IDs/timestamps and does not fabricate sent messages or mark old cards newly unread.

First-version Review request and View session links open existing authorized decision/detail surfaces. Sending text, viewing a card or accepting message consent never confirms capacity, accepts a connection or changes a booking. Other candidates' identities, counts, status or content are absent even when they request the same occurrence.

### 16.3 Removal, terminal sessions and blocking

Removing a connection revokes its general-message grant and any earlier general consent for that directed pair. Existing history stays readable. A fresh eligible message request and recipient acceptance may resume general chat without reconnecting; fresh connection acceptance also restores that grant. A request/booking status becoming terminal does not erase or automatically close the ongoing service conversation. Independently authorized inquiry/request/booking communication remains available after removal. Removal is therefore not a ban on all contact; **Block** is a separate action.

A block is owner-controlled and applies to both communication directions between those two account profiles in the dataset, including a reverse teaching relationship. Blocking removes existing accepted connections and terminalizes pending connection/message-consent attempts without deleting evidence. New inquiries, general messages, message requests, connection sends/acceptance, private-session requests and pending private-request acceptance fail while either participant's block remains active. Existing pending booking requests may still be withdrawn/declined/expire normally; block never silently confirms or cancels a booking. Existing booking cancellation/completion and independently authorized session-note history remain available under their original rules.

The copied booking exception is limited: when a block is created, capture the exact shared confirmed bookings whose scheduled end is still future at that instant. Their participants may send messages explicitly attached to those bookings before/during the session and until its immutable scheduled end plus 14 × 24 hours. A later completion does not extend that bound. Cancelling a booking ends its exception immediately; other captured bookings are evaluated separately. A booking already ended/cancelled at blocking, a pending request, a new inquiry or a newly invented context cannot bypass the block. Replaying a block cannot extend a window. With multiple active blocks, the context must qualify under each. Database time decides the boundary; history remains readable afterward.

Only a blocker may remove their own block. Unblocking restores current eligibility checks, not removed connections, terminal attempts or revoked general consent. New inquiry contact may again be available for a currently visible eligible coach, while general messages require current accepted connection or fresh message-request consent. Blocking cannot promise to hide already-public coach information from anonymous visitors and adds no group-registration rule.

An approved coach hiding their public profile stops new public inquiries but retains existing authorized conversations. Coach-application suspension prevents new inquiry/general outreach and leaves existing confirmed-booking coordination under the same limited scheduled-end-plus-14-days default; it does not grant access to unrelated content. An authentication/account access revocation denies that actor reads/writes; retained counterpart history follows its existing authority. Account-deletion retention remains DEV0165 and is not inferred from Airbnb.

### 16.4 Message bounds, recovery and freshness

These are adopted MovX implementation defaults, not additional user decisions or claims about Airbnb:

- Plain text with preserved line breaks, explicit Send and append-only messages initially. Reject blank/invalid text; each body has at most 4,000 Unicode code points and 16,000 UTF-8 bytes, inside a 32 KiB request envelope. No edit/delete/unsend, attachments, rich text, typing, recipient-visible read receipts, automated replies or notification delivery is required.
- Paginate at most 20 conversations and 50 entries per response with stable cursors and deterministic sequence/ID ordering. History remains reachable beyond the first page. Counterpart presentation is bounded name/initials and already-authorized public or relationship media; inquiry/message consent grants no private avatar, email or broader athlete record.
- A sender-generated submission ID is unique per author/conversation. Exact retries recover one saved message; altered-body replay fails rather than replacing it. Uncertain responses retain the authorized draft and submission identity until reconciliation. Actor switch or access revocation clears old content/drafts. Message bodies/drafts never enter logs, analytics, public caches or cross-account persistent browser storage.
- Enforce database-backed user-text limits of 20 sends/minute per actor/conversation and 100/minute per actor/dataset. Limit first-contact inquiries/message requests to 10 new counterpart pairs per actor per rolling 24 hours, with one pending general consent attempt per pair and a 24-hour retry delay after recipient decline. Existing booking-card/status persistence is not user text and must not impose a candidate cap or prevent a legitimate booking request. Concurrent limit/send and retry decisions use database time and stable identities.
- The badge counts conversations with unread incoming content, not message totals, connection requests or pending booking decisions. A recipient-owned read-through position advances only through entries actually rendered in the active visible conversation. A racing unseen entry stays unread. A pending message request is visible in a distinct Message requests view; reading it is not acceptance. Reading a booking card does not resolve the coach's decision.
- Read on entry, on focus/visibility restoration and every 30 seconds while visible. Coalesce overlapping reads, stop polling while hidden and reconcile stable identities/revisions without replacing drafts, moving focus or allowing an old response to overwrite a newer state. A failed read shows an explicit unavailable/stale result, not a zero badge or empty inbox. No instantaneous-arrival promise is made.

The user confirmed on 15 October 2026 that this first implementation uses server-managed PostgreSQL message storage and does not adopt Airbnb’s five-year deletion rule. No automatic time-based purge or one-year thread closure is required. Retained history is not a promise of indefinite retention; account-deletion/retention work remains DEV0165. The message bodies are application-readable under restricted database/server authority, not end-to-end encrypted.

### 16.5 Authority and transaction boundaries

PostgreSQL enforces participant/dataset scope, pair uniqueness, immutable context references, one pending consent attempt, exact-recipient decisions, read ownership, idempotency, message limits and block exceptions. Browser role labels, selected cards and submitted counterpart IDs never grant access. Use deterministic pair/context locks so send/block/remove/accept/read races return the actual committed result and a stale attempt cannot accept a newer one.

Saving a booking request and its unique conversation card is one database transaction. Acceptance, cancellation and other scheduling transitions expose their canonical persisted status and idempotent activity without a partial message-side success. Connection accept/remove/block integration uses the same pair guard and consent generation; no UI-only revocation or deletion/recreation resets the history. Maintain current occurrence/booking concurrency constraints and no candidate cap. Database migration coverage must include fresh installations, upgrades with retained request/booking/connection history and safe history bootstrap. No legacy blockchain/social schema is repurposed.
