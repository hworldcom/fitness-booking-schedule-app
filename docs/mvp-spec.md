# MovX Club — Scheduling, messaging and group-training MVP specification

Last updated: 19 October 2026.

MovX is a focused scheduling product for martial-arts and fitness coaching. Guests discover platform-approved coaches and their published availability counts; fictional demo coaches are available only in local runs. Email-authenticated athletes request one capacity-one private session, and the coach confirms one athlete before a booking exists. Approved coaches manage their public profile, training locations, location-aware effective hours, reusable private/group session types, request queue and booked schedule. Typed private classes offer 60, 75 or 90 minutes; untouched legacy inventory retains its one-hour model.

The delivered scheduling foundation is extended by the adopted group-training target in section 15: direct registration, manual coach confirmation and Free or pay-at-session pricing. The full Events flow is not delivered yet. This project excludes blockchain, wallets, tokens, passes, credits, in-app payment collection, group funding, follows, posts and feeds. Historical excluded-product records do not restore those capabilities; Solana hackathon work belongs to the separate `fitness-booking-social-app` repository.

The adopted M8 [private-messaging target](#16-private-messages-and-session-notes) adds coach inquiries before requesting a session, ongoing pair conversations containing request cards and normal coach chat. Its runtime and local two-account validation are delivered under COR0022; hosted release remains separate. DEV0248 additionally delivers shared session titles and session-filtered message history. The [session-note contract](#17-session-training-plans-and-notes) is adopted under DEV0182; note persistence and interfaces remain separate, undelivered COR0015 work.

### Proposed implementation defaults

The following are implementation defaults rather than additional user decisions:

- Home uses canonical discipline URL state and a fixed four-coach preview. Unsupported and retired query/location/page parameters redirect to that discipline-only URL. Supported case/alias normalization such as BJJ remains. Explore more preserves the selected discipline and opens the default complete Explore view; alphabetical directory ordering and Explore query/primary-place matching remain unchanged.
- M9 materializes effective private availability for the current Europe/Berlin date and the following 29 local dates, excluding starts at/before database time. The complete operating defaults, including overrides and restoration, are in section 18; DEV0252 implements this backend authority while calendar/editor/public-navigation peers remain pending.

5. Closing the occurrence marks it withdrawn and closes all pending requests as unavailable in one transaction. It does not edit the recurring availability rule or create a booking.
6. A request that reaches its decision deadline expires without consuming capacity. That deadline is normally 12 hours after submission but is capped at occurrence start for a late request. Concurrent accept/decline/withdraw/expire/close operations resolve to one authoritative idempotent outcome.

### 4.5 Cancel a booking

1. The owning athlete or coach selects a future confirmed booking.
2. One authorized operation records the cancellation actor/time and releases the reservation. Reopen only if the occurrence still satisfies the effective availability, horizon and coach-wide conflict checks in section 18; a retained booking on a day off must not reopen public time after cancellation.
3. Repeating the same cancellation returns the same terminal outcome; a different or unauthorized actor cannot mutate it. Reopening the occurrence never revives earlier request attempts, so athletes must submit new requests while it remains eligible.

### 4.6 Complete a session

1. After the scheduled start, the owning coach can mark a confirmed booking completed.
2. Completion is terminal and idempotent. Athletes and unrelated coaches cannot complete it.
3. P0 does not claim independent attendance proof or resolve service disputes.

2. In Schedule → Availability, the coach edits weekday switches and whole-hour time ranges with one active owned venue per range, copies days deliberately and composes the complete weekly draft locally.
3. One explicit authorized save validates ownership, active-location state, the expected baseline and per-coach time overlap before replacing the complete recurring rule set and synchronizing effective dated occurrences once. Date overrides replace selected whole dates across venues; booked sessions remain with a warning. Section 18 defines closure, restoration and conflict behavior.

## 5. Scheduling state model

### Application profile

One profile belongs to one verified Supabase Auth identity inside a dataset. It stores the bounded display identity used for authorization and presentation.

### Coach application and review

One current coach application belongs to one application profile. Absence means not applied; submitted applications move through `pending`, `approved`, `rejected` or `suspended`. Review events are append-only evidence of platform decisions. Historical self-service activation may remain stored for continuity but grants no current coach authority.

### Coach profile and training locations

Supported coaching disciplines are Boxing, Muay Thai, Kickboxing, Brazilian Jiu-Jitsu, MMA, Wrestling and Fitness. Fitness covers general strength and conditioning within the same scheduling contract; it creates no nutrition or medical-advice capability. One owner-scoped coach profile contains public name, slug, biography, disciplines, visibility, one scheduling timezone and an optional public portrait reference. It owns one or more confirmed provider-neutral training-location records; each record has stable identity, kind, public label, coordinates, provenance and active/inactive lifecycle, and may optionally reference a reviewed gym. Exactly one active location is primary while the profile is visible. That primary record supplies the coach's single Explore marker and public training-base label, but it does not establish live presence, choose an occurrence venue or grant gym/account authority.

Pending/rejected applicants may keep only a private draft; a user-created profile enters public discovery only while its application is approved, its owner selects visible and one confirmed active primary location exists. Provider-search selection stores only the bounded coach-owned location the coach explicitly confirms and does not populate a global point-of-interest catalogue. Referenced locations are deactivated rather than destructively deleted so scheduling history remains resolvable.

### Profile images

Image bytes live in Supabase Storage rather than PostgreSQL or the Vercel deployment filesystem. PostgreSQL stores only bounded source/path/version metadata. Private account avatars and public coach portraits use separate storage/access contracts; attaching an image never grants account or coach authority. Signed-in account presentation—including the desktop bottom-left account control, upper-right header control and Profile—prefers its private avatar and otherwise may display the owner's already-public coach portrait as a read-only fallback. A regular account therefore shows its private avatar or initials, while a coach can additionally fall back to the public portrait. Athlete `My sessions` cards may project the booked coach's current public portrait and link its complete visible identity to the existing public coach profile; a missing, removed or unreadable portrait retains initials. The owning coach may retrieve a request participant's current private avatar through a request-scoped authenticated application route for as long as that request record is retained, including terminal recent-request history. The owning coach of a confirmed booking may separately retrieve that booking athlete's current private avatar through a booking-scoped route while the booking remains confirmed. Public callers, athletes and unrelated coaches receive no image bytes, and the browser never receives the private Storage object path. These fallbacks and bounded reads do not copy media between buckets or change which endpoint removes each image.

JPEG, PNG and WebP source selections are accepted for the first release. The browser corrects orientation, square-crops and downsizes before upload; the server treats that output as untrusted, validates and re-encodes one canonical 512 × 512 WebP at quality 82, strips metadata and retains no original. The selected source is limited to 8 MiB and the canonical upload to 512 KiB. Replacement writes a new immutable object path before retiring the prior owned object so content-delivery-network caches do not retain an overwritten image. Removal restores the initials fallback.

### Coach–athlete connection

A connection is a private lasting relationship for one coach-profile/athlete-profile pair within the same dataset. A request records the sender and recipient; only its recipient accepts or declines, and only its sender withdraws. Its lifecycle is `Pending → Accepted`, `Pending → Declined` or `Pending → Withdrawn`; either accepted participant may transition `Accepted → Removed`. A fresh request may reconnect an eligible pair after a terminal outcome, while old attempts remain terminal. Crossed sends return the existing pending request rather than implying consent.

Pair identity, participant IDs, request/attempt identity, lifecycle state, creation/update/decision timestamps and decision/removal actor are the minimum authority data. PostgreSQL enforces one active pending/accepted pair and rechecks a shared confirmed/completed booking at sending and acceptance. A later cancellation never removes an already accepted connection. A different teaching direction between two coach accounts is a distinct pair; self and cross-dataset pairs fail closed.

Accepted projections contain only participant identity, bounded current presentation and sessions shared by that exact pair. The coach receives an authenticated connection-scoped opaque URL for the athlete's private avatar. Pending/declined/withdrawn/removed connections grant no new private-media or session-history access. Removal revokes the connection permission on the next read, with caching that cannot preserve former access; separate request/booking permissions remain intact. Goals, biographies and public athlete profiles are not added. Connection-only delivery does not implement messaging; M8 defines its separate contact permissions and still promises no notification delivery.

Disconnecting is a relationship transition, not a content-deletion operation. A fresh accepted attempt for the same pair restores access to a complete bounded history view of retained shared requests/bookings and, after COR0015 delivery, their booking-bound shared plans and notes. M8 conversation history is retained independently of connection state; current ordinary-message authority follows section 16. Retrieval may be paginated but must permit access to all retained records rather than silently dropping older history. Each request/booking and conversation keeps its original stable identifier, authors, timestamps, status and permissions; reconnection neither reopens terminal records nor copies content into a new request or booking. Independent participant access survives disconnection. Recovery includes unsuccessful request cards and cancelled-session content, excludes coach-private and unrelated content, and does not bypass qualifying-session/coach eligibility or future separately adopted account-retention policy.

### Recurring availability and dated occurrence

A recurring weekly rule belongs to exactly one active training location owned by the same coach and creates deterministic dated one-hour occurrences in the coach's single scheduling timezone. One weekday/time cell belongs to at most one location. An occurrence is `open`, `booked` or `withdrawn`, carries the immutable snapshot of the rule's actual location and belongs to exactly one coach. An open occurrence may have multiple independent pending requests without being held or booked. Overlapping rules/occurrences are rejected across all of the coach's locations. Changing which location is primary does not change a rule or occurrence. M9 adds versioned whole-date overrides and distinguishes availability-policy closures from manual occurrence closures; effective hours, replacement identities and recovery are defined in section 18. Section 18.6 extends this legacy occurrence model with virtual 60/75/90-minute typed candidates. Typed open candidates may overlap because pending requests reserve no capacity; confirmation, held/booked intervals and group reservations remain exclusive.

### Booking request

A booking request is one athlete's attempt to receive coach confirmation for one occurrence. The same athlete has at most one active request for that occurrence, while terminal attempts remain immutable history and do not prevent a later eligible attempt. There is no per-occurrence candidate cap; private coach queries use bounded pagination. Its lifecycle is:

```text
Pending -> Accepted
Pending -> Declined
Pending -> Withdrawn
Pending -> Expired
Pending -> Not selected
Pending -> Occurrence closed
```

`Accepted` creates and links exactly one confirmed booking. Accepting one request makes every other pending request for that occurrence internally `Not selected`; closing or otherwise withdrawing the occurrence makes them `Occurrence closed`. Athlete presentation uses neutral `Not confirmed`, `Request closed` or `Session unavailable` language and never describes competition. All other terminal outcomes consume no capacity. Minimum request fields are request/attempt ID, occurrence/coach/athlete IDs, scheduled time and actual location snapshot, status, creation/update timestamps, decision deadline capped at occurrence start, decision actor/time and an optional accepted-booking ID. The athlete projection contains only that athlete's attempt and never exposes other request identifiers, identity, count, status or content.

### Private booking

A private booking is created only when the owning coach accepts a pending request and binds that occurrence, coach profile and selected athlete profile. Its lifecycle is:

```text
Confirmed -> Cancelled
Confirmed -> Completed
```

Minimum projection fields are booking ID, occurrence ID, coach/athlete IDs, scheduled start/end, actual occurrence-location snapshot, status, creation/update timestamps, cancellation actor/time and completion time. It contains no wallet, pass, credit, token, price, transaction signature or chain reference.

### Session training record

The adopted note target adds at most one shared coach-authored plan plus separately scoped shared and coach-private entries per original private booking. Records retain booking/dataset identity, author, original creation time, update time and optimistic revision. They are neither messages nor connection-owned athlete records. See [section 17](#17-session-training-plans-and-notes); runtime remains DEV0183–DEV0185.

### Group session and registration

M7 introduces separate group activity, registration and coach time-reservation records. Their lifecycle, frozen terms and atomic authority are defined in [section 15](#15-group-training-and-events); they do not reuse private booking IDs or the historical funding projection tables.

### Private conversation and historical contact

M8 keeps one dataset/coach-profile/athlete-profile conversation, stable message and booking-request card identities, recipient read positions and pair-level blocks. Earlier message-consent attempts remain historical audit records; the app offers no approval workflow. Context-specific send authority and the block/booking exception are defined in [section 16](#16-private-messages-and-session-notes). Messages neither establish a connection nor allocate a session. COR0015 session-content records remain booking-bound.

## 7. Requests, booking and recovery

Request creation, withdrawal, decline, expiry, acceptance, occurrence closure, booking cancellation and completion are atomic database operations with stable UUID identity and explicit actor context. Start/deadline checks use database instants, not the browser clock. The active-booking uniqueness rule prevents double confirmation even when several requests exist or coach actions race.

A request is eligible only when the occurrence is open, belongs to another visible coach, starts after authoritative database time and falls within the effective 30-day publication policy in section 18. No fixed minimum notice applies because only coach acceptance assigns capacity. Creation fixes its deadline at `least(created_at + 12 hours, scheduled_start_at)`, so a late request never remains pending after training begins. The Berlin-only interface renders these instants in `Europe/Berlin`, including daylight-saving changes, without changing stored authority.

A repeated same-athlete submission returns that athlete's active request. A terminal unsuccessful attempt—withdrawn, expired, declined, not selected or closed with the occurrence—remains history but permits a new attempt while the occurrence is still open and eligible. No candidate cap applies. Coach request queries are paginated, and athlete projections never contain another request or aggregate candidate count.

Acceptance locks the occurrence and relevant requests in deterministic order, creates one confirmed booking, marks the selected request accepted and occurrence booked, and closes every other pending request as not selected. Coach occurrence closure uses the same concurrency boundary to withdraw that occurrence and close its pending requests without selecting anyone. Decline closes one attempt; withdrawal belongs only to its requesting athlete; bounded reconciliation expires overdue requests. Every mutation returns the state that actually won rather than false success.

Cancellation locks the confirmed booking/occurrence together, records the authorized actor and frees the reservation. It reopens only a future occurrence still allowed by the current effective hours, actual venue, publication horizon and coach-wide conflicts; a day off or replacement venue cannot be bypassed by cancellation. It does not revive earlier request attempts; interested athletes submit new requests if the reopened occurrence is still open and future. Completion locks the booking and requires the owning coach plus an elapsed scheduled start. Terminal request and booking transitions are idempotent and cannot be reversed.

Connection send/accept operations recheck exact participants, current coach capability and a shared confirmed/completed booking inside the database mutation. Booking cancellation and connection acceptance must serialize their qualifying-state check so a race cannot accept a pair that lacks a qualifying booking at the winning acceptance instant. Duplicate/crossed sends, replayed decisions and accept/remove races return the authoritative attempt/pair state; stale request identifiers cannot accept a newer attempt. Connection actions never alter session capacity, booking state or request eligibility.

For M8, a saved request and its unique message card are committed or recovered together. Ordinary messages require no separate approval or connection acceptance. Blocking and concurrent send/request/accept/connection decisions serialize through a common participant-pair guard; booking-related exceptions use immutable booking identities and database time. See section 16 for exact scope. No mail integration failure may leave a successful request response with a missing card.

## 10. Security and failure invariants

- Actor context is derived from a verified session and set transaction-locally.
- Direct table permissions remain least privilege; security-definer functions validate actor context and exact identifiers.
- Coach approval/rejection/suspension is unavailable to ordinary runtime roles. Decisions are expected-state guarded and atomically append immutable review evidence.
- Editable profile fields, Auth metadata, URL parameters and historical activation timestamps never establish approval or badge state.
- Private capacity-one enforcement exists in PostgreSQL, not only in browser button state; group maximum capacity and per-athlete active registration are separate database constraints.
- One active request per athlete/occurrence and one confirmed booking per occurrence are enforced in PostgreSQL, not only in interface state.
- Acceptance and occurrence closure lock the occurrence and relevant requests deterministically so concurrent decisions cannot produce two winners or partial closure.
- Cross-run/cross-coach location identifiers, hidden coaches, self-requesting, elapsed/non-open occurrences and invalid lifecycle transitions fail closed.
- PostgreSQL enforces one active primary location for a visible coach and prevents overlapping active occurrences across all of that coach's locations; browser colors, selectors and marker state are not authority.
- Mutation errors return bounded messages without database/Auth internals.
- Browser inputs are normalized and bounded; server/domain validation is authoritative.
- Uploaded images reject unsupported types and oversized/malformed data, are decoded and re-encoded server-side with metadata removed, and use immutable owner-scoped object paths. Browser-reported names, types and dimensions are not trusted.
- Private request/booking queries are actor-scoped and reveal only necessary identity/display/time/location/status fields. Athlete responses contain no other-candidate record or aggregate count; unbounded candidate lists are never returned in one response.
- Hosted builds reject loopback/incomplete database/Auth configuration without printing values.
- Pair uniqueness, explicit recipient consent and qualifying-booking checks for connections are enforced by PostgreSQL, including cancellation/acceptance races.
- Connection-derived private data/media is revocable on removal, and bounded projections reveal neither another pair nor private coach-note metadata.

### 15.1 Confirmed choices and implementation defaults

Confirmed by the user: coaches create dated sessions with minimum/maximum participants; athletes discover and join; the initial model supports Free and paid-at-session sessions; joining reserves a seat directly, while the owning coach confirms or cancels manually. The following are adopted implementation defaults, not additional user decisions:

- Minimum and maximum are integers with `1 <= minimum <= maximum <= 100`. ### 15.2 Coach create, publish and decide

The owner drafts a title, bounded description, discipline/intended level, owned venue, local date/start/end, decision deadline, minimum/maximum and Free or pay-at-session price. Publication rechecks current approval/visibility, active owned location, future valid times and cross-product schedule conflicts, then stores frozen reviewed terms and the actual venue snapshot. A draft is not public inventory and blocks no time.

A published session is initially **Waiting for coach confirmation** and reserves coach time while collecting registrations. The owner can confirm only while the minimum is currently met and database time is before the decision deadline. Reaching the threshold only enables that action. Confirmation stays stable if later withdrawals lower the count. The coach can cancel a future open or confirmed session; cancellation terminates every active registration once and releases group time.

No explicit owner decision before the deadline yields **Not confirmed** regardless of participant count. The outcome is terminal; the owner cannot revive it after the deadline. Existing participant history remains available with truthful cancelled/not-confirmed/elapsed labels. An elapsed activity is presented as **Ended**; this is not proof that training took place, payment occurred or anyone attended.

### 15.3 Joining, leaving and privacy

Guests can view published upcoming activity details, actual venue/time, the decision deadline, fixed pricing, coach identity/trust label and aggregate active-registration counts. A guest signs in by the existing email flow before joining. One account may reserve one active seat per session; the owner cannot register for their own session, while other coaches can participate as athletes through their ordinary account. Current coach eligibility/visibility, dataset, time and capacity are rechecked in the mutation.

### 15.4 Data and atomic lifecycle

New scheduling-owned group records store stable IDs, dataset/owner, frozen published terms/venue, event state, active capacity, decision/cancellation actor/time and bounded revision/timestamps. Ended is an elapsed presentation of retained history, not a coach attendance/completion mutation. Registrations transition from Active to Withdrawn, Session cancelled or Session not confirmed, retaining earlier attempts. Direct registration and session confirmation are distinct operations.

PostgreSQL enforces maximum capacity, one active registration per session/account, actor/dataset scope and terminal outcomes. Join/withdraw/confirm/cancel/deadline reconciliation locks the same session and affected registrations in a deterministic order. A confirm-versus-withdraw race either confirms with the minimum met at the winning instant or reports the actual under-minimum state; once confirmed, a later withdrawal does not reverse it. Exact retries are idempotent; stale attempts cannot mutate a replacement registration. No browser count or timer is authority.
